Stroke-Related Cognitive Impairment

By- Santanu Maity (39)

Stroke is out when the blood supply to part of the brain is broken off or when a brain’s blood vessel gives out. As the brain controls many functions in the body, stroke can play out in how you move as well as how you think. Some patients do have issues with attention, memory, visual processing, problem solving, and coordination post stroke. For example a person may put forth effort to recall recent events, have a hard time with focus during a task, or delay in response to what they see. Also they may have trouble with hand use or the body’s ability to go with what the eyes see. Cognitive issues from stroke also present different in each person. Some people mainly have problems with memory, while others may have more difficulty with attention or decision-making. These problems can also change during recovery.
Attention problems may make it difficult for a person to concentrate on one activity for a long time. They may become distracted easily or have trouble following instructions with several steps.
Memory problems are also common. A person may have difficulty remembering names, instructions, appointments, or information they have recently learned. Sometimes they may remember something after being given a small reminder.
Visual processing can also be affected. A person may have difficulty noticing objects, understanding where things are located, or following a moving object. This can present a challenge for some every day activities. After a stroke problem solving and decision making may become a slow process. A person may take more time to assess a problem and determine a solution. Also routine activities which require planning and organization may become a struggle. Co ordination issues may also play a role in how a person controls their movement. For example reaching for an object, using a computer mouse, or moving from one place to another may require more effort. These issues may in turn affect a person’s independence and daily living. But which is the case for many, improvement is possible especially with appropriate rehabilitation and regular practice. Cognitive rehab often focuses on specific areas which include attention, memory, visual skills, and problem solving.
Simple rehabilitation exercises can be made into games so that the activities are easier to practise repeatedly. A game can also record basic results such as accuracy, reaction time, and progress over several attempts.
The main idea of this project is to create a simple game that focuses on one or more cognitive abilities affected after stroke. It should be easy to understand and should not be treated as a medical test.
Figuring Out:-
1. Pattern Memory Game:-
A short sequence of letters, numbers, or objects appears on the screen for a few seconds.
For example: A S D F
The sequence then disappears and the player has to enter or select the same sequence.
The sequence can become longer in each round.
Measure:
Memory
Accuracy
Number of mistakes
Progress between attempts
This game mainly focuses on memory and attention.
2. Target Finding Game:-
Different shapes or objects appear at random positions on the screen.
The player has to find a particular target, such as a circle or a specific symbol, as quickly as possible.
The target can change in every round.
Measure:
Reaction time
Accuracy
Missed targets
This can be used to practise attention and visual processing.
3. Visual Tracking Game:-
A small target moves around the screen.
The player has to follow the target using a mouse or keyboard.
The movement can start slowly and become slightly faster in later rounds.
Measure:
Tracking accuracy
Reaction time
Coordination
This activity focuses mainly on visual attention and hand-eye coordination.
4. Signal Response Game:-
Different signals appear on the screen, but the player should respond only to a particular signal.
For instance the player will hit a button when a green circle is present but should ignore other shapes. In some levels of the game the player is presented with more distractors which increases the difficulty. Measure:.

Reaction time

Correct responses

Missed signals

Attention
